# Project handover — start here

Last updated: 2026-10-18. Current phase: production v3 deployed to the private API with the corrected managed-cycle scan accounting (scan method `managed-cycle-scan-v4`), now run as a supervised systemd user service, plus nine scheduled evidence jobs on the server. A Regional evidence tab shows source-checked records from the complete IBO 2023 to 2026 reports beside the weather results, place search covers 5.2 million towns and villages worldwide, and the Season ribbon and Every winter tabs show the modelled season for all winters at a glance. Since 2026-10-13 the app is served from the lab server at http://fpt-server.ad.ufl.edu:8789/ to the UF network and VPN, without a login. The roadmap is `docs/research/2026-10-11/source_expansion_and_improvement_plan.md`; its Phase A is mostly done. An evergreen planner that starts from the pruning date is proposed in `docs/research/2026-10-18/evergreen_expert_review.html` for expert review; it is a plan, not approved and not built. Primary profile `stage_thermal_v3`, with a 50-hour chill requirement since 2026-10-12; methods `open-field-production-v3` / `location-evidence-v5`. **Global daily and land-hourly acquisition are complete and connected to the private API. The v3 stage constants are provisional, and the benchmark compares with regional literature, not field observations. Phenology validation, soil acquisition and the broader core handoff are not complete. Since 2026-10-12 the planting window is Paul's R rule, counted back from modelled budbreak; its offsets are unsourced and unvalidated. Risk-gate refinements, cool-climate constants and bias correction wait for decisions. GitHub publication is authorized; public hosting is not set up.**

  - **Research findings (2026-10-15, no code change).** The scan assumes a crop can start on any date. The literature says evergreen flowering needs short days or cool temperatures after shoot growth stops; in 'Misty', 28 °C gave 8.6 flower buds per plant against 38.0 at 21 °C. Budbreak comes about 15 days after pruning, and first harvest 138 to 175 days after pruning on the Peruvian coast and 167 to 193 days in Brasília. Bloom to first harvest takes 51 to 129 days, while the v3 heat rule gives about 46 days at Chao. In Villacurí, pruning 12 days earlier brought harvest 10 days earlier; in Michoacán, prunings 8 weeks apart gave first harvests about 10 days apart. No published tool sets an evergreen pruning date.
