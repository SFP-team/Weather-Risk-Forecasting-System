Shade nets: 35–40 % nets delay harvest ~3 weeks and increase berry size (Chile, 'Brigitta'); nets change canopy T and RH but published °C offsets are net-colour-specific and small; a 60 % net had **no** effect on root-zone soil T under a closed canopy with frequent irrigation (Machado, Bryla & Correia 2023 Acta Hort 1357:55 https://doi.org/10.17660/ActaHortic.2023.1357.55). Not enough for a parameter; keep as a note.

### 1.2 Pots / substrate

- Substrate: composted pine bark, peat, coir at pH 4.5–5.5; container production "not limited by suboptimal soil… control substrate pH, drainage" (Li & Bi 2019; Fang et al. 2020). UF trial: no effect of five bark/coir/perlite mixes or 40 vs 50 L pot on growth in first two years (Fang, Nunez, Fisher, Munoz, SSRN 4021904).
- Water: pine bark has "little water buffering capacity; frequent irrigation events are required" (ISHS Acta 779_14). Nanjing pots were dripped 4×/day with ≥20 % leaching. Waterlogging risk is removed; dry-spell risk becomes an irrigation-reliability risk, not a weather risk.
- Root-zone heat: blueberry roots grow poorly in warm media; Évora soils >45 °C; black pots gave larger canopy than white-wrapped pots with no yield loss after one year (Machado et al. 2023). Nursery literature: black container substrate up to ~17 °C (30 °F) above air on sunny days; black pots 6–7× more hours >38 °C than white (Agriculture 15:2185, 2025 https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture15212185); root growth ceases ~38 °C, direct injury with ≥30 min at higher T (Nursery Management/Mathers). Blueberry-specific pot-T thresholds not found [unverified].
- Root frost: pot root ball approaches ambient air (no soil buffering); cranberry roots lethal at −20 °C while shoots survive −30 °C (ISHS 1440_38). Blueberry fine-root damage thresholds (−3 to −9 °C) come from grower forums only [unverified]. Irrelevant at all current SE-US/Brazil sites (cold_days ≤ 18/yr, Tmin rarely < −5 °C) but should be flagged for global use.
- Pine-bark beds (ground) lower flower-level air T by up to 2.8 °C (5 °F) on calm, dry nights (HS216) — a ground-system, not pot, penalty.

### 1.3 Frost protection and hydrogen cyanamide

- Overhead irrigation: 0.2 in/h (5 mm/h) protects to 18 °F (−7.8 °C) with no wind; fails at 26 °F (−3.3 °C) with 15 mph (HS216 Table 1, Gerber & Martsolf 1965). Smith 2019 (Agriculture 9:9): 6.3 mm/h protects to −2.8 °C at 0–16 km/h. Unprotected early SHB in Gainesville averaged >60 % loss over many years; most FL growers have overhead systems. Side effects: ice load, Botrytis, leaching. Feb 2026 event exceeded system capability (HS216, rev. Sep 2026).
- Wind machines: ≤50 % of inversion strength (~1–3 °C), radiative freezes only; harmful in advective freezes (UGA B1479; Agric. For. Meteorol. 2019).
- Hydrogen cyanamide: deciduous system only, after ~300 h chill, before flower-bud stage 2; 0.75–1 % advances vegetative budbreak and shortens FDP by up to 1.5 weeks; 1.5–2 % injures flower buds, worse on unchilled plants. "Not compatible with the evergreen or organic production systems" (Williamson FSHS 2002; HS1362; FBGA blog).
- What the tool should say: frost protection is a **feasibility note** (dew point, wind, water volume) attached to the freeze risk, never a reduction of the risk (Paul decision 4: do not prioritise feasibility). HC is a **calendar note** for deciduous sites with ≥300 h (shortens budbreak→bloom; not for evergreen); never modelled as chill.
