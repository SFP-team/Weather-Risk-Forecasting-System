# Second opinion: crop-modelling / horticultural-climatology view

## 1. Findings with sources

### 1.1 Perennial-fruit suitability and phenology frameworks

**chillR / Dynamic Model / Safe Winter Chill (SWC).** Luedeling's comparison of chill models across 5,000 stations found Chilling Hours the most temperature-sensitive and least transferable between climates; the Dynamic Model (chill portions, CP) is the recommended metric, especially in warm winters, because it accounts for chill negation by warm hours and counts the 7–12 °C band ([Luedeling & Brown 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3077742/); [Luedeling 2012 review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304423812003305)). The chillR manual states the author does not recommend Chilling Hours "especially in warm climates" ([chillR PDF](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/chillR/chillR.pdf)). Utah units go negative in warm regions and should be dropped. **SWC** = the 10 % quantile of the winter-chill distribution, i.e. chill exceeded in 90 % of years, the largest chilling requirement a grower can rely on; Luedeling computes it from 100 synthetic winters generated by a weather generator (RMAWGEN) fitted to the observed record, precisely because 15–30 real winters give an unstable 10th percentile ([Luedeling 2009](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0006166); [Fernandez et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-021-03276-w)). The project's own data check already shows the mis-ranking (Papanduva 183 h but 26 CP ≈ Waldo; CH/CP ratio falls from 13.5 in Georgia to 4.5–7 in south Florida/Brazil; `RISK_FACTOR_RESEARCH.md` §2).

**PhenoFlex** (Dynamic chill + Growing Degree Hours + sigmoidal chill→heat transition, 12 parameters) is fitted directly to bloom-date series with hourly temperature; calibration/validation split ≈ 75/25 or leave-one-year-out; reported RMSE ≈ 3.8–4.0 d for apple/pear, and errors grow to 5.5 d when marginal warm winters are included ([Luedeling et al. 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016819232100174X); [Fernandez et al. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016819232200209X); [vignette](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/chillR/vignettes/PhenoFlex.html)). Key lesson: it needs ≥ 20–30 bloom-years per cultivar; it fails at the warm margin, which is exactly where this project operates. Uncertainty is reported as RMSE in days on held-out years, plus bootstrap of the fitted chill/heat response curves.

**Sequential/parallel chill–heat models (Utah/Anderson, Richardson)** are the older families; blueberry work uses the sequential form. For northern highbush, combined chill + GDD linear models predict bloom onset with cross-validated R² > 0.75 about 30 d ahead ([Shope et al., preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7217434)); NHB bloom stages fit logistic curves on GDD with base ≈ 7–8 °C ([Kirk & Isaacs 2012](https://journals.ashs.org/hortsci/view/journals/hortsci/47/9/article-p1291.xml)). For SHB in Florida the only bloom-GDD numbers are Kovaleski 2015 (Emerald ≈ 225, Jewel ≈ 302 GDD base 7 from 1 Jan to 50 % bloom; already in the research doc). No published SHB model reports RMSE; no UF cultivar has a published CP requirement [unverified beyond Emerald's 100–400 h label, [HortScience 2008](https://journals.ashs.org/hortsci/view/journals/hortsci/43/5/article-p1606.xml)].

**Wine-grape indices (Winkler GDD, Huglin, GST)** are climatological season-sum indices used with fixed class bands (e.g. Winkler regions I–V). Their transferable idea: a *single, well-documented seasonal index with published class cut-points and a stated station basis*, not a fitted phenology model. The project's evergreen/semi/deciduous classifier is the same kind of object and should be presented the same way: index + cut-points + evidence level, with the cut-points re-expressed in CP.

**What transfers:** (a) CP as primary chill metric; (b) SWC/p10 instead of mean; (c) hourly interpolation from daily Tmin/Tmax (chillR `stack_hourly_temps`, Linvill sine/exponential) so the *global* daily cube can yield CP without a global hourly cube; (d) validation = held-out bloom years with RMSE in days; (e) uncertainty = per-winter distribution plus model-structure spread (report CH and CP side by side until a decision).

### 1.2 Climate analogs (Paul's §5, §12)

The CCAFS analogues framework (Ramírez-Villegas et al. 2011) assumes "all baseline conditions in the two sites are generally similar and the only important difference is the climate" ([CCAFS WP-12](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/57a08ad5e5274a31e00007da/ccafs-wp-12-climate-analogues-web.pdf); [cgspace](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/handle/10568/16420)). Best current practice (Mahony et al. 2017; applied to US specialty crops by [Chaudhary et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-35887-x)) is: choose *agriculturally relevant* metrics (chill, GDD, frost, seasonal rain), scale by *interannual variability* (Mahalanobis, reported as sigma-dissimilarity), and use analogs to *catalyse dialogue*, not to rank suitability. Paul's implementation (lines ~98–112, 349–405) uses hand-set variable weights on WorldClim monthly normals, Euclidean distance, and a median-bandwidth Gaussian similarity; then `final_suitability_score = 0.70·similarity + 0.30·(100 − risk)` (line ~1408). Problems: (1) weights are untested judgement; (2) monthly normals carry no interannual variability, so a site that matches Citra on means but has 3× the freeze variance scores identical; (3) phase alignment by calendar month ignores photoperiod, which for evergreen SHB is a real driver; (4) mixing similarity 70 % into suitability lets an analog score override modelled risk. The literature does not support analog similarity as a suitability component. **Verdict: drop analog similarity from any score or ranking; keep, at most, a 'closest reference cell' descriptor computed as sigma-dissimilarity on the project's own risk metrics (CP, bloom-freeze days, harvest rain), labelled exploratory.**

### 1.3 Climate change

**Data.** NEX-GDDP-CMIP6: 35 GCMs, SSP1-2.6/2-4.5/3-7.0/5-8.5, 1950–2100, daily tasmin/tasmax/pr/hurs/rsds, 0.25°, BCSD bias-corrected to GMFD; free on AWS S3, NCCS THREDDS (OPeNDAP subsetting), Earth Engine ([Thrasher et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01393-4); [NCCS tech note](https://www.nccs.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/NEX-GDDP-CMIP6-Tech_Note.pdf); [AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/nex-gddp-cmip6/)). Daily only → chill requires hourly interpolation (chillR method) and delta-change against POWER's baseline so the two datasets' biases cancel.

**Published projections.** Southeast US rabbiteye (RCP8.5, 20 CMIP5 GCMs, 1981–2000 → 2041–2070): chilling hours −66.6 %, chill portions roughly −50 %, later onset and shorter chill season, freezes still occurring in critical windows ([bioRxiv 2025](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.08.663765v1), preprint, [unverified peer review]). Florida FAWN: all stations show declining chill 1999–2018, Sebring mean 108 h, Gainesville 503 h ([FBGA/Fraisse](https://www.floridablueberrygrowers.org/index.php?option=com_dailyplanetblog&view=entry&category=blueberry-blog&id=99:chill-accumulation-trends-in-florida-are-we-heading-toward-warmer-winters-)). South America: Fernandez et al. 2021 mapped SWC in CP for 158 stations (1980–2017) and future scenarios; southern Brazil is already marginal and central Chile loses most chill ([Climatic Change 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10584-021-03276-w)). Peru's coast is a zero-chill evergreen system where the binding risk is heat, not chill; growers are replacing cultivars after a heat-driven −25 % season ([National Geographic 2025](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/peru-blueberry-industry-boom); [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9593919/no-chill-blueberry-varieties-are-key-to-maintaining-production-levels-in-peru/)) [trade press; unverified]. No peer-reviewed blueberry-specific CMIP6 projection for Florida/Georgia/Brazil/Peru was found; the rabbiteye preprint and Fernandez 2021 are the closest.

**Should the tool add it?** Yes, as one scenario card (2040s, SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 ensemble median + 10–90 % model spread) showing ΔCP, ΔSWC, Δbloom-freeze days, Δfruit-heat days. It answers the breeding program's actual question (which class of cultivar to breed for a site in 15 years) and the literature repeatedly shows that a chill-hour projection exaggerates loss relative to CP, so this card only makes sense *after* the CP switch.

## 2. Concrete recommendations (file level)

1. **`production.py`**: add `chill_definition='dynamic_cp'` using the ChillModels/chillR constants (a 30-line function); add `safe_winter_chill` (p10) and `chill_fulfilment_fraction` per winter to the summary; keep `bounded_0_7_2` as comparator. Re-express `evergreen_chill_max`/`deciduous_chill_min` in CP (provisional ≈ 8 and 24 CP from the §2 table, labelled *derived from this project's cells*).
2. **`production.py` PROFILES**: replace the 50 h anchor with cultivar-class profiles (SHB-low, SHB-mid, RE) whose bloom is GDD-from-1-Jan/1-Jul with 10/50/90 targets; harvest offsets per species. Emit `evidence_level` per date.
3. **New `pipelines/weather/bias_correction.py`**: monthly quantile mapping of daily Tmin/Tmax per stored cell against a co-located station; store correction tables; `production.py` consumes corrected series with a `corrected: bool` flag in the row.
4. **New `pipelines/weather/hourly_from_daily.py`**: chillR-style hourly interpolation (sunrise/sunset from latitude) so CP can be computed from the global daily cube; validate on the 19 hourly cells (report CP RMSE).
5. **Risk ranking** (`production.py` EVENTS): add severity fields (degree-hours below stage threshold, mm above threshold) and apply Paul's overlap-and-frequency gate; rank by severity × frequency, show P(event) with CI as secondary.
6. **Sample depth**: extend the POWER pull to 1981 for the 19 cells first (cheap), globally later; recompute p10.
7. **Hourly source**: evaluate ERA5-Land hourly (9 km) as the on-demand hourly source; run the same station comparison as POWER; adopt if bias is lower.
8. **New `pipelines/weather/future_climate.py`**: NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 point extraction (OPeNDAP subset), delta-change to POWER baseline, CP/SWC/freeze/heat deltas for 2040–2069, SSP2-4.5 + SSP5-8.5, ensemble median and 10–90 % range; one card in the UI.
9. **Analog outputs**: do not port §5/§12 similarity into `location_api.py`. If Paul wants a hint, compute sigma-dissimilarity on risk metrics against the 14 reference cells and label it exploratory.
10. **Validation harness** (`tests/weather/` throwaway → `docs/`): collect UF bloom/harvest dates for Citra/Waldo/Papanduva that the breeding program already holds; report bloom RMSE in days per profile. This is the one thing that turns 'provisional' into 'validated'.

## 4. Risks, unknowns, human decisions

- **Chill metric decision (Paul, open).** Literature is unambiguous for CP, but UF growers and AgroClimate speak chill hours; recommend CP primary, hours displayed as comparator. Decision needed on CP class cut-points, which have no published SHB basis [derived from project cells].
- **Cultivar-class requirements**: no published CP requirement for any UF/UGA cultivar; profiles will be provisional until Gerardo/Patricio supply bloom records. Human decision: which cultivars define SHB-low/mid.
- **Station access**: FAWN is public; Brazil INMET public; Georgia UGA network public. Bias correction beyond the 19 cells requires a decision on whether to apply a regional offset globally.
- **NEX-GDDP-CMIP6** daily-only and BCSD-to-GMFD: use delta-change, not absolute values; ensemble spread must be shown. Rabbiteye projection is a preprint.
- **Analog outputs**: Paul authored them; dropping them from the product is a supervisor decision. Recommend presenting the sigma-dissimilarity alternative as the replacement.
- **Evergreen systems** (Peru, S-FL) are management-driven; the chill chain does not apply and heat/VPD is the binding risk; the literature offers no validated evergreen phenology model [gap].
- Cross-topic: bias correction (rec 3) changes every risk count; sequence it before ranking work. The CMIP6 card depends on rec 1 and 6.
